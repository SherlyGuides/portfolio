Learno: from content player to teaching assistant

The situation. Learno started as a player for lesson content. Teachers were running hands-on build classes with a room full of students, a projector, and school internet that came and went.

What I did. I sat in classrooms and delivery sessions and watched where teachers lost time. Every feature involved at least one teacher's feedback and was agreed with the founders and stakeholders, then shipped and tested fast. I shipped about two features a day with Claude Code.

  • Offline sessions, so a class doesn't stop when the Wi-Fi does.
  • Phone-number sign-in, because teachers don't remember another password.
  • Parts lists teachers tick off during a build.
  • Plain-English activity logs for reviewers who aren't technical.

What I cut. Anything that gave the teacher one more thing to operate. Shipping fast only works if you remove just as fast.

Teacher readiness, run on WhatsApp

The situation. Teachers at ~100 schools need to be ready before a lesson, and they already live on WhatsApp, so readiness went there instead of into another app.

What I did. Built readiness into the chat they already use: prompts go out on WhatsApp, teachers can answer by voice, progress is tracked per school, and anyone falling behind is escalated to a person on our side.

Why it matters here. This is the same shape as getting a small business paid: an automated nudge in a familiar channel, a record of who has responded, and a human step when automation stops working. The Vyapar idea below builds on it.

Production pipelines the company runs on

The situation. Every lesson needs slides, animated video, 3D models and printed books.

What I did. Automated each stream and connected them. Books are now produced end to end: AI writes the content and images, Python handles formatting, and image-to-text models review the pages. Lesson content is checked automatically for reading level, terminology and safety rules. I also created a 3D (GLB) content stream the company didn't have before.

If I joined

A first look at Vyapar: reminders that get paid

A short product note in the format I would bring to the team: problem, what I'd check first, a sketch, and how we'd know it worked.

Written from the outside, using only public information about Vyapar. Everything here is a hypothesis to test with real users and real data, not a conclusion.

What's already there

  • Over 1 crore businesses use Vyapar for billing, stock and accounts.
  • Bills and payment reminders can be sent on WhatsApp, with UPI collection.
  • Billing works offline and syncs later, and the app runs in 9+ languages.
  • The Suvit acquisition (Nov 2025) adds AI-led reconciliation and compliance.

The problem I'd bet on

For a kirana store or a distributor, udhaar (selling on credit) keeps regular customers coming back, and it's also where cash gets stuck. Sending a reminder is already one tap. Getting paid is harder: owners hold back from chasing regulars because they don't want to spoil the relationship, so the reminder never goes out, or goes out once and stops.

What I'd check in the first two weeks

  1. Spend a working day each in about ten shops and distributors across two Bangalore markets, especially at closing time when the day's dues get settled.
  2. Pull reminder data: what share of reminders leads to payment within 7 days, and does that change with the UPI link, language or time of day?
  3. Talk to support and sales about why owners switch reminders off.

The idea: a reminder ladder

Vyapar handles the polite, repetitive follow-up. The owner only steps in when a personal call is actually needed, and the customer can answer "I'll pay Friday" with one tap. That promise is recorded and followed up automatically.

Guardrails: customers opting out or blocking the shop, and owners switching reminders off. If those rise, the ladder is too pushy.

What I'd leave out of v1

  • Automated calls and anything that sounds like a legal notice.
  • Late fees or interest. Owners decide that face to face.
  • New screens. The ladder lives inside the existing reminder flow.

How I'd roll it out

Start with owners who already send reminders by hand, compare collection against a matched group for a month, then widen.

How I work

The same four steps, every time

Step 1Sit with the user

In their classroom, shop or workshop, during a normal day. Not in a meeting room.

Step 2Ship the smallest useful thing

Agree it with the people who own the outcome, then build it in days, often myself.

Step 3Watch it get used

Go back and see whether it saved time or added a step. Check the numbers.

Step 4Cut what doesn't earn its place

Every feature has to justify the attention it takes. Most products need fewer.
