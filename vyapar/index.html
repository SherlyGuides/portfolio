Learno

Learno started as a player for lesson content. Teachers use it in hands-on build classes, usually with a projector, a full room and unreliable internet. It's now closer to a teaching assistant.

Most of what I know about it comes from sitting in classes and delivery sessions. Every feature involves at least one teacher's feedback and is agreed with the founders and stakeholders.   • Phone-number sign-in.
  • Parts lists teachers tick off during a build.
  • Activity logs in plain English for reviewers who aren't technical.

Teacher readiness on WhatsApp

Teachers at ~100 schools need to be prepared before each lesson. They already use WhatsApp all day, so I built readiness there instead of in a separate app. Prompts go out on WhatsApp, teachers can reply by voice, progress is tracked per school, and anyone falling behind gets escalated to someone on our team.

It works a lot like payment reminders do at Vyapar, which is why I picked that for the note further down.

Content production

Each lesson needs slides, animated video, 3D models and printed books. I automated these: about 70% less effort on PPTs and 95% on animated videos. I also started 3D (GLB) content, which the company didn't have before.

Books go through one pipeline: AI generates the text and images, Python does the formatting, and image-to-text models review the pages. AI-generated lesson content is checked automatically for reading level, terminology and safety rules.

Contract workers outside company retainers now run the pipeline, so it adds nothing to fixed payroll.

Vyapar

An idea: following up on unpaid bills

I only have public information about Vyapar, so this is a starting point for a conversation, not a plan.

What Vyapar already has

  • Over 1 crore businesses using it for billing, stock and accounts.
  • Bills and payment reminders on WhatsApp, with UPI collection.
  • Offline billing that syncs later, in 9+ languages.
  • Suvit, acquired in Nov 2025, for reconciliation and compliance.

The problem

Shops and distributors sell a lot on credit (udhaar). Sending a reminder is already easy. My guess is that the harder part is following up: owners don't want to keep chasing regular customers, so reminders go out once and then stop.

What I'd check first

  1. Spend a day each in around ten shops and distributors in Bangalore, especially at closing time.
  2. Look at what share of reminders lead to payment within 7 days, and whether the UPI link, language or time of day changes that.
  3. Ask support and sales why owners turn reminders off.

The idea

Let Vyapar send the routine follow-ups on a schedule, and only bring the owner in when a call is needed. The customer can reply with a date they'll pay, and Vyapar reminds them on that day.

I'd also watch how many customers block the shop and how many owners turn reminders off. If those go up, the reminders are too pushy.

Not in the first version

  • Automated calls, or wording that sounds like a legal notice.
  • Late fees or interest. That's for the owner to decide.
  • New screens. It should fit into the existing reminder flow.

Rollout

Start with owners who already send reminders, compare their collections with a similar group for a month, then expand.
